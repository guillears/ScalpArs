# EXH_EXIT2: can a break-even, lock, late trail, small TP or time exit beat today's EXHAUSTION_SHORT exit? (2026-11-18)

**BACKTEST only.** Research only: no engine, config or test change, nothing committed.
Pre-registered in `EXH_EXIT2_2026-11-18_PREREG.md` (frozen 22:31 UTC, before any cell was simulated). HOLDOUT was read once.

## Verdict: **KEEP today's exit** (TP min(20 × ATR15, 10 %) maker / SL min(1.5 × ATR15, 6 %) / 24 h)
- **None of the 39 new exits survives on DESIGN.** Every cell's paired Δ against today's exit is ≤ 0.
- The pre-registered rule says no survivor means KEEP. HOLDOUT, E and the joint book all agree. No cell is an observe candidate either: no cell has HOLDOUT Δ > 0 with even half of the bar.
- **Break-even and profit-lock moves are refuted for this sleeve.** They save the stopped trades that went our way first, but they cut more of the +10 % winners. Those winners also dip back to the entry before they run.
- **Small TPs (1–3 %) with tight stops (2–4 %) are refuted.** They lose 0.5–0.8 % a trade against today's exit, in all 7 half-years.
- The only near-zero cell is the no-progress exit (NP). It fires on 0.2–6 % of trades and changes nothing: Δ −0.00 to −0.02.
- **There is nothing to haircut:** the best Δ is −0.005 %/trade.

## Question
EXHAUSTION_SHORT stops out on 57 % of its trades. Many of those went our way first (GAVE_BACK).
- Can a break-even move, a profit lock or a trailing stop bank that move without cutting the big +8.7 % winners?
- Would small fixed TPs with tight stops, or shorter holds, do better?

EXH_EXIT_GRID already tested trailing stops armed at +2 to +5 %, TPs of 4–10 %, stops of 4–6 % and 12 / 48 h holds. Every trail lost there. This study tests only what it did not cover:
- break-even moves;
- profit locks;
- trails armed after +6 % (so they cannot touch a trade before it is well on the way to the TP);
- trails with no TP, to let runners go past 10 %;
- small TPs with tight stops;
- holds under 12 h;
- a no-progress exit.

## Method
- **Entries (frozen):** the 3,368 kept fills of `BASELINE_TRADES_EXHAUSTION_2026-11-18.csv` (dl582_block False), 2023-07 → 2026-11-18.
  - Every exit runs on the same entries (paired).
  - Caveat: a shorter exit could free the coin for a later signal. That is not added. No cell holds longer than 24 h, so no cell blocks an entry today's exit took.
- **Engine:** the NOSPOT_EXIT 1m engine (`scripts/nospot_exit_sim.py`), the same one DIST_EXIT uses, so the methods match. It is extended only with per-cell ATR / fixed TP and SL modes and the NP exit (`scripts/exh_exit2_sim.py`).
  - 1m futures paths from data.binance.vision (1,570 new pair-days, 0 API weight). 99.97 % of trades ran on 1m.
  - Within a bar, the stop as it stood at the bar open is checked first, on the high. The maker TP is checked next and fills only when the low trades 1 tick through it. A bar that touches both counts as a stop.
  - Stops fill at max(stop, bar open), so gaps are charged.
  - Break-even, lock and trail levels move only after the bar and apply from the next bar.
- **Costs:**
  - Taker fee 0.05 % on the entry and on every stop, break-even, lock, trail, time or NP exit. Maker fee 0.02 % on a TP, with no slip.
  - Slip on every taker leg = max(0.10 %, −0.042 + 0.276·√(6,100 ÷ q5)): the size model at 0.61 × a $10k account, with a 0.10 % floor.
  - Real funding.
  - Stress: a flat 0.20 % per taker leg.
- **Parity:** the reference reproduces the baseline file exactly: same exit kind on 3,368 / 3,368 trades and gross r equal to 1e-10. Every mechanism cell shows Δ = 0 on every path that never reached its arm (0 exceptions).
- **Splits:** DESIGN = 2023-07 → 2025-06 (1,349 fills) · HOLDOUT = 2025-07 → 2026-11 (2,019 fills) · E = 2023-H1, the clean block (248 candidates → DL 582 block → 215 fills after one position per pair).

## What it means
- The 57 % stop rate is not a leak that a lock can plug. Three quarters of the stops do go our way first, median +2.5 %. But at +2 to +4 % a future +10 % winner and a future give-back look the same, and the same coins dip back to entry before either outcome.
- Banking early pays on a random entry and costs on an exhaustion entry. That confirms the edge is the slow bleed after the exhaustion (EXHAUSTION_EXPAND: "the state is the edge"), and today's wide maker TP is what harvests it.
- What would help is an **entry-side** read that separates the give-backs from the winners at entry, not an exit that guesses after the fact. That points to the path-label W/L (`sweep_separators.py EXHAUSTION_SHORT --label path`), not to more exit cells.

## Caveats
1. **BACKTEST.** DESIGN and HOLDOUT were read before for the entry rule, and the exit L2 was chosen on 2025-01 → 2026-06 (EXH_EXIT_GRID). For exits these are robustness reads; only E is clean. The reference is the incumbent, so any selection bias favours REF; nothing came close enough for that to matter.
2. **Paired entries:** shorter exits do not get extra entries from freeing a coin earlier. A short exit could add some signals, but on the random control those extra entries would be near breakeven at best.
3. **1m intrabar order is unknown;** the stop is checked first. Locks and trails update on the next bar, which is mildly optimistic for them.
4. **Maker TP queue position is not modelled** beyond the 1-tick trade-through rule. This favours REF and the S cells equally.
5. **The random control** uses the real fill's ATR15 and q5 for its random bars in the same episode (±24 h). 0.1 % of its trades ran on the 5m fallback.
6. **E's DL 582 block** uses OI from data.binance.vision metrics (the last 5m row ≤ entry) ÷ the 24 h futures quote volume from 5m bars. This is the same definition as the engine, with a different data source.
