# LITE: can we tell, at the +1 % touch, which trades will die? (2026-10-15)

Research only. No engine, config, template or test file was changed; nothing committed; no bot API call.
Pre-registration `reports/LITE_AT1_PREREG.txt` (frozen 21:27 UTC, input hashes) + addenda A1 (tick-only verdict, path shape, classifier), B (tiers, side-first 3D, every variable, named volume axes; part (c) T2-vs-T3 dropped by the operator) and C (a tier-label bug fix, declared before any screen output existed).
New data: coin 1m klines **with taker-buy volume** for every cohort fill (2,063 public calls, budget respected, no 418/429), stored in `reports/lite_at1/k1m/` (hashes in `fetch_manifest.txt`; 2 delisted symbols returned nothing → flow features missing on 5 fills).
**This study has not been reviewed. It supports no arm and no ship.**

## Plain-English answer

**No. At the moment a LITE trade first reaches +1 %, nothing we can measure tells the future "dies at −3" trades apart from the ones that go on to +2 / +3 — not price speed, not path shape, not coin or market volume, not taker flow, not RSI / ADX / EMA gaps re-computed at the touch, not any of the 207 entry variables, alone or in pairs, inside or outside the C zone. A trained model does no better than a coin flip on the other year.**

What the data says, in order:

1. **Most LITE trades do touch +1.** Tick-priced fills: 2025 191 / 264 (72 %), 2026 472 / 624 (76 %).
2. **Most of those go on to +2 under C.** Of the trades still open when the +1 minute closes, the ones that never reach +2 (the operator's "T1": DUSK, ZK) are **22 % (2025) and 16 % (2026)**. The other 78–84 % reach C's target.
3. **The T1 trades are real money.** Each one books about −3.2. Spread over every LITE trade they cost **−0.50 %/trade (2025) and −0.35 %/trade (2026)**. That is the prize if they could be spotted perfectly.
4. **Banking at +1 is a bad trade unless you can spot them.** Banking at the touch minute's close books about **+0.72–0.80**. A trade that goes on books about +2.0 (+2.1 inside the zone). So banking only pays in a group where **more than ~25 % of the touches would die**. The base rate is 14–23 %.
5. **No group reaches that bar out of sample.** We tested about 4.1 million rules on the tick-priced set (8.2 million including the all-fill refute line): side-first, every variable, 1D + every 2D pair, 7 policies. The best rule on each side is no better than what day-shuffled noise produces:
   - Inside the zone: family-wise p 0.38.
   - Outside: p 0.55.
   - At entry: p 1.00 inside, 0.13 outside.
   - In walk-forward, **0 of 160** discovered rules confirmed in the other year (t ≥ 2). The top-1 rule's held-out Δ was positive in only 3 of 8 runs, none with t ≥ 2 (best t 1.3).
   - The best single variable at the touch, outside the zone, scores only 0.62 (the noise ceiling is 2.5).
6. **Unconditional rules lose clearly in 2026.** "Bank +1 always", "stop to break-even / +0.3 / −1 at +1" and "trail 0.5 / 0.8 / 1.0 after +1" all cost **−0.21 to −0.37 %/trade in 2026**, with 95 % day-bootstrap intervals entirely below zero. In 2025 they are flat (−0.09 to +0.07, intervals straddle zero).
7. **Master and live point opposite ways, and both samples are tiny.**
   - On the 4 live trades, "bank at +1" would have helped: −2.35 → +1.84. Two of the four were T1 (DUSK, ZK).
   - On the 20 master trades it would have hurt: +7.34 → −7.67, because 9 of the 10 acted master trades went on to +2 / +3 (8 of them to +3).
   - Master + live together: C +4.99 vs bank −5.83.

**Bottom line for the operator:** "+1 % is too much to drop" is true on DUSK and ZK, but for every DUSK/ZK there are about 4–5 trades that look the same at +1 and go on to +2 or +3. Banking +1 on all of them costs about a third of a percent per trade (2026). We can't yet tell them apart with any data we have. The C ship (TP +2 outside the zone) already handles the EPIC case: EPIC reached +2.59, and C books +2.03 there instead of −3.43.

**Recommendation:** keep C unchanged. No observe-only line is proposed, because no cell earned a frozen gate. What could still find the duds is data we don't have (blind spots below), mainly the order book at the touch and aggressor-side ticks.

## 7. Blind spots (what could NOT be tested)

1. **Order book at the touch** (depth, spread, book imbalance). There is no historical book data. The live entry_book_* stamps exist only on live fills (0 % on the backtest).
2. **Aggressor side at tick resolution.** Tick prints carry no side, so taker-buy share is only available per 1m bar (5m aggregated from it).
3. **LITE_GVOL24_LOW's 24 h dashboard market volume.** It is not rebuildable from the cache for 2025 (it needs the dashboard's top-N 24 h volumes). entry_frenzy_gvol and entry_global_volume_ratio stand in for it.
4. **Sub-minute decisions.** Policies act at the close of the touch minute, except P1, which is a resting limit at the touch itself. A tick-level "decide at the touch print" variant was parity-checked against the exit walker but is not reported as a separate screen.
5. **Open interest, funding at the touch, liquidations.** Not in the cache. entry_funding_rate was scored at entry only.
6. **2025 non-top-50 fills are 1m-priced.** They are a refute-only line. Their at-touch features use 1m bars.

## 8. Method and parity (every headline two ways)

- **Pricing.** C and every policy are priced on the menu's hybrid path (tick prints while covered, then 1m), with resting-maker TP, stop-first on an ambiguous bar and −0.2 slip on stop / cap / market exits.
  - C equals `reports/lite_wlsweep/menu.csv` on **1,472 / 1,472** fills, and P1 equals menu M1_3 on 1,472 / 1,472.
  - Stop-move / trail with the decision at the touch event equal `study_lite_exitopt_common.walk_loop` on 1,476 / 1,476.
- **Recomputed BTC 1h EMA20 slope vs the entry stamp:** r 0.970.
- **Exit-counterfactual rule.** Fills that never touch +1 while C is open have Δ = 0 by construction. The 73 touches (all blocks) where C already exits inside the touch minute are also Δ = 0.
