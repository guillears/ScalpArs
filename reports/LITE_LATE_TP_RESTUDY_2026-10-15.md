# LITE: does any time-based exit rule make sense? Duration + late-TP re-study (2026-10-15)

Research only. No engine, config, template or test was changed. Nothing was committed. Cache-only: no Binance call was made.
The late-exit grid was pre-registered (reports/LITE_LATE_TP_PREREG.txt, frozen 2026-10-15 15:05:55Z) before any cell was priced.
This study has not been reviewed, so it supports no arm or ship.

## Plain-English summary

**Verdict: keep today's LITE exit (+3 / −3 / 12 h). No time rule is a candidate.**
- 54 rules were tested: 6 times (30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240 min) × 9 actions (close; lower the TP to 0 / 0.5 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 2.5; tighten the
  stop to −1.5 / −1).
- Not one is better in both backtest years with confidence, under real trade prices or under the resting-limit TP the bot now uses.
- Only one rule is even slightly positive in both years on trade prices: "TP → +2 after 30 min", at +0.04 (2025) and +0.006 (2026)
  %/trade. That is far from the bar (P 0.81 / 0.53). On the master trades it loses −0.33 %/trade, and under the resting-limit TP it is
  negative in 2026.

**What the trade durations say**
1. **LITE trades are slow, and winners and losers take about as long.** The median win takes 35–50 minutes and the median stop 49–52
   minutes. About 40 % of trades are still open after 1 hour and about 20–26 % after 2 hours (P1–P2 below). The 12-hour cap almost never
   fires (1 %).
2. **A trade still open at minute X is still a coin flip.** Of the trades still open at 30, 60, 120 or 240 minutes, about 43–53 % still
   reach +3 and about 45–53 % still get stopped, in every period (P3).
3. **Holding on beats closing, on average.** "Hold value" is what holding to today's exit earns compared with closing at minute X.
   - 2026: +0.26 at 30 min, +0.10 at 60, −0.01 at 120, +0.45 at 240.
   - 2025: −0.08 at 30, then +0.11 to +0.31 from 60 min on.
   - A close at X is also a market order, which gives back about 0.2 more in live trading.
   - So closing early cuts winners about as often as it saves losers. That is the whole result (two-sided table: at 30 min, "close"
     saves 293 losers worth +728 points but kills 241 winners worth −678 in 2025; 2026: +440 vs −528).
4. **Why "lower the TP after T" does not work.** A trade that is up +1 to +2 at minute 30 still reaches the full +3 about 71–76 % of the
   time. Lowering the TP sells exactly those trades early. The trades it rescues (negative at T, later bounce to the lower TP, then
   stop) exist, but they are about as many as the ones it cuts.
5. **Why "tighten the stop after T" does not work.** Two to four hours in, a trade sitting between −1.5 and 0 still reaches +3 about a
   third of the time (32–39 %, P4), so a −1 / −1.5 late stop cuts those too. Every late-stop cell is about +0.01 to −0.08 on trade prices.

**What the 2026-10-14 first pass got wrong, and what changed**
- **Its 2025 block had only 7 fills because of a loader bug, not the cohort.** The script read only the 2026 data cache. The 2025 tick
  and 1m data live in a separate cache (backtest_cache_2025), which the tick-study loader patches add. Without them, 821 of 828 2025
  trades were "unresolved" and silently dropped.
  - Fixed here: all 828 2025 trades are walked (264 top-50 with ticks, 564 on 1m bars).
  - With the full year, every 2025 number of the first pass flips. Its "+1.16 for C60" came from 7 trades (5 stops).
- **Its 2026 numbers reproduce exactly** on the same 624 trades: close at 30 −0.1512 (ref −0.151), TP → 1 at 30 −0.0949 (−0.095),
  TP → 1.5 at 60 +0.0363 (+0.036).
- Its best cell, TP → 1.5 after 60 min, is not a candidate:
  - 2025 −0.039 (P 0.18) · 2026 +0.036 (P 0.74).
  - Concentrated: one day (2026-04-12) carries 43 % of its 2026 gain and one pair (LAUSDT) 53 %; pooled, one day is 60 % of ΣΔ.
  - Master −0.225.
  - Resting-limit ruler: 2025 −0.027 / 2026 +0.017.

**Is there a market-regime switch?** 24 cells win in one year and lose in the other. For those, all 25 regime variables stamped at entry
were read (sign split, day units).
- There is a coherent direction. Cutting the TP or closing early helps when BTC is falling at entry (BTC 1-day return < 0, BTC 1 h slope
  < 0) and hurts when BTC is rising.
- It is not convincingly stronger than chance: 205 flags vs a day-shuffled null of 132 on average (p 0.10).
- It is a hypothesis only. Do not act on it. The input (entry_btc_1d_ret_pct) is already stamped on every fill, so the two-year W/L
  study can re-read it for free.

**Master and live**
- **Master B18–B21 (20 trades).** Pooled, the master Δ is ≤ 0 in 53 of 54 cells (only T60_SL1.5 +0.04): close at 30 −0.13, TP → 2 at 30 −0.33, TP → 1.5 at 60

- **Live (1 trade, DEEP).** It hit TP at 12 min, before the earliest T (30), so every rule is identical to today (Δ = 0 by construction).

**Recommendation (operator decides).**

- The useful live measurement is the same as for WILLY: is the resting +3 TP filling, and do stops fill at or better than the line?
  The resting-TP shadow (DL 295 / 296) already logs this.

## Could NOT be tested

- **Live block.** N = 1 (DEEP), with no path in the cache. Its recorded exit (12 min) is before every T, so Δ = 0 by construction.
- **2025 non-top-50 (564 fills) are 1m bars only.**
  - On bars, a TP books at its line, a stop at the line or the bar open, and a bar touching both lines counts as a stop.
  - The 1m share flatters early cuts. T30 cells are positive on 2025 1m rows but negative on 2025 tick rows. Examples (S0):
    - T30_TP1.5: rest +0.112 vs top-50 −0.088.
    - T30_CLOSE: rest +0.087 vs top-50 −0.014.
  - G5 catches this. The 2025 positives of the T30 cells are mostly this artefact.
- **Queue position of a resting late TP.** RM assumes a fill once the price trades ≥ 1 tick through. A post-only re-place race at T is
  priced as "already crossed → market at the next print".
- **Slot effects.** An earlier exit frees one of LITE's 2 slots for another LITE entry. This is not modelled.
- **Regime read.** 25 entry_btc_* / breadth / volume stamps at ≥ 90 % coverage. The breadth entry_br_* and entry_bear_* columns are empty
  in the replay.
