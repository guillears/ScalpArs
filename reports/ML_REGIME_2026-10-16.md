## Plain-English answer

**1. What was different between the three eras: the coins' 5-minute pops faded more often in 2025. The difference is the same everywhere you look.**
- The sleeve did not get bigger wins in 2026. Average win (+0.37 / +0.42 / +0.44 %) and average loss (−0.82 / −0.83 / −0.79 %) barely moved.
- What changed is **how often the stop was hit**: 43 % of fills in 2025, 36 % in H1, 33 % in H2. WR rose 53 → 59 → 63 %.
- **The improvement shows up in every slice.** H2 beat 2025 in **29 of 29** slices: pattern cell, 4-hour block, ATR tercile, ADX bucket, pricing method, downtrend or not, BTC 24 h up or down, and each of the top-15 coins. H1 beat 2025 in 26 of 29.
  - Under sleeve-kill checklist ③ that is the signature of a **market-wide cause**. It does not point at an entry rule, a coin or an hour.
- **The coin mix does not explain it.** A regression with 23 entry and coin variables (ATR, ADX, RSI, coin age, volumes, rank, slopes, BTC ADX/RSI, funding, downtrend, BTC24H, pricing, hour) leaves the gap *larger*: +0.17 becomes +0.21.
- **Backtest pricing does not explain it either** (unlike LITE).
  - 2025 tick-priced fills average −0.177 against −0.187 for all fills, so 1-minute pricing explains only about 0.01 of the 0.17 gap.
  - Every 2026 fill is tick-priced.
- **About 0.05 of the 0.17 gap is BTC's path in the 15 minutes after we bought.** The other 0.12 is the coin itself.
  - Over all 5-minute moments, BTC's 15-minute drift is about zero in every era (+0.02 / −0.22 / +0.38 bps).
  - Right after our entries it was −1.1 bps (2025), −1.2 (H1) and **+3.3 (H2)**. In 2025 and H1 our entries tended to land just before BTC dipped; in H2, just before it rose.
  - **Even with the same BTC path, 2025 was worse.**

    | what BTC did after entry | 2025 | H1 | H2 |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | BTC flat | −0.19 | −0.08 | −0.04 |
    | BTC up | +0.07 | +0.13 | +0.20 |

**2. Was it knowable in advance? No.**
- **We tested 50 day-level variables**, each measured at 00:00 UTC from earlier data only:
  - 19 are new trend-persistence measures (the direct "do pushes continue?" variables):
    - BTC 5-minute autocorrelation and variance ratios over 1, 3 and 7 days;
    - BTC 15-minute push follow-through;
    - BTC intraday efficiency;
    - the median top-50 alt's variance ratio, autocorrelation and "does a 5-minute pop continue for 15 minutes".
  - 4 measure volatility, and 3 are the sleeve's own recent results.
  - 24 are the LITE regime set: BTC trend and moving averages, distance from the 30-day high, breadth, alt-vs-BTC, dispersion, volume, funding and FRENZY activity.
- **Nothing beats chance.**
  - Best family-wise p is 0.20 to 0.96 per era. Pooled across eras (with each era's own level removed) it is 0.79.
  - 8 of 50 variables point the same way in all three eras, against 11.6 expected by chance.
- **The theory's own measures point the wrong way at the era level.**
  - Persistence was *not* higher in the good era. H2 had the most mean-reverting 5-minute markets: alt autocorrelation −0.029 vs −0.019 in 2025, BTC variance ratio 0.950 vs 0.962.
  - No variable's shift between eras can account for more than 17 % of the gap (best: funding, which is not even measured in 2025).
- **Good and bad periods do not persist.**
  - Weekly autocorrelation is +0.15 / +0.21 / −0.07 (p 0.11 / 0.10 / 0.50).
  - Month-to-month differences are within noise in 2026 (p 0.48 / 0.33) and borderline in 2025 (p 0.056).
  - A good week does not tell you the next week will be good, so **no on/off switch can work**.

**3. Is anything usable? No regime switch.**
- **The closest candidate is the theory's own primary, `btc_vr3_7 > 1`** (BTC 15-minute moves were trending rather than mean-reverting over the past week). It fails the gate:
  - It points the right way in all three eras (+0.02 / +0.04 / +0.20).
  - It picks the better days in all 6 learn-on-one-era / test-on-another directions, and in master (+0.18, P 0.91).
  - But the pooled p is 0.38. The shuffle null for "6 of 6 directions" has p 0.17.
  - The dose-response is U-shaped, not monotonic (by the rules, that points to a confound).
  - The good side is rare: 13–27 % of fills, and only 0 of the 29 fresh fills. In 2025 the kept side still loses (−0.17).
- **The pre-registered selection, `spk24` (many FRENZY spikes yesterday), fails.**
  - In 2025 it keeps the *worse* days (selectivity −0.12), and it also fails in master (−0.08).
- **In the tick-priced-only run, `br_up3` passes the nominal p (0.042), but not the bar.**
  - Family-wise it is 0.86, and in 2025 it picks the better days only by chance (selectivity −0.006).
- **A warning for anyone reading these tables later:** because momentum longs lose in every backtest era, **any** skip rule "saves money" there. 17 of 50 variables "save %" in all 6 directions for that reason alone.
  - This study therefore judged every switch on **selectivity**: average of the fills kept minus average of the fills skipped. On that score, nothing survives.
- **Conditional entry effects (Q3).** Splitting by regime does not make the small effects consistent. Two of them are already consistent *without* any split:
  - **ADX falling at entry:** −0.07 / −0.07 / −0.12 %/fill in the three eras, pooled p 0.001, 34–38 % of fills. This is the existing ADX observe line (DECISION_LOG 273).
  - **BTC24H_DOWN:** −0.03 / −0.07 / −0.20, pooled p 0.05. This is the existing BTC24H_DOWN line.
  - "ADX 20–30 inside a downtrend" is −0.01 / −0.10 / −0.33. It is sharper on high-spike days (−0.17 pooled), but that split itself failed, so it is a descriptive cell only.
  - **None of these is a new line.** They belong to their existing frozen gates.

**Recommendation (operator decides):** no regime switch, no sizing by regime, no new Scout line.
- **What the data says:** momentum long's edge depends on whether coin pops keep going for 15 minutes. In 2025 they faded more often across the board. Nothing we can measure before the day or week starts saw that coming, and the good and bad stretches do not persist long enough to switch on.
- **Where to look next.** These are untested causes that could produce a uniform shift:
  1. Order-flow and crowding data we do not have for 2025: open interest, liquidations, long/short ratio, order-book depth. A market where 5-minute breakouts are sold into by liquidity providers would look exactly like this.
  2. A competition / microstructure change between the years. This cannot be tested with 3 eras: three eras are three observations.
  3. A post-entry rule rather than a pre-entry one. The trade is decided by BTC in the first 15 minutes, which the earlier BTCFWD exit studies covered.

## Q0 · Theory (written before the data, prereg §4)

A 5-minute momentum long buys a coin that has just shown strength (EMA stack, ADX rising), with a small TP ladder and a tight stop. It needs the strength to **continue** for 15–60 minutes before noise reaches the stop.

**It should work when short-horizon returns persist:**
- positive autocorrelation, variance ratio above 1, pushes that continue;
- a calm or steady volatility level rather than a rising one (transitions are choppy);
- broad participation, so alts follow BTC up.

**It should fail when short-horizon returns mean-revert:** liquidity providers fade pops, stops get hit first.

**Measurable day-level versions:**
- BTC and median-alt 5m/15m autocorrelation and variance ratio over the prior 1–7 days;
- "does a pop continue for 15 minutes" (BTC pushes; alt top-decile 5-minute bars);
- BTC intraday efficiency;
- realised volatility and its change;
- breadth, dispersion and volume trend;
- funding;
- the sleeve's own recent results.

**Primaries (single tests, predicted +):** `btc_vr3_7`, `alt_ft_7`, `btc_ac5_3`.

| primary | pooled Δ 1 d (p) | by era 2025 / H1 / H2 | sign held? |
|---|---|---|---|
| btc_vr3_7 (BTC 15-min variance ratio > 1, 7 d) | +0.047 (0.38) | +0.017 / +0.040 / +0.198 | yes, not significant |
| alt_ft_7 (alt pops continued, 7 d) | −0.077 (0.15) | −0.114 / −0.120 / +0.278 | **no** (opposite in 2 of 3) |
| btc_ac5_3 (BTC 5-min autocorrelation > 0, 3 d) | −0.036 (0.39) | −0.080 / +0.061 / −0.133 | **no** |

## Method notes

**Features** (`scripts/study_ml_regime_features.py` → `reports/ml_regime/day_features.csv`):
- Every value for day d uses UTC days ≤ d−1 only.
- The alt universe is the LITE DEV1 top-50 by 30-day average quote volume. 396 distinct alts were used over the period.
- The L-family (LITE features) is missing on 2026-10-16. That affects 4 fresh fills, only for L variables.

**Deviations** (declared):
1. The pricing flag is the replica `src` (see Q1).
2. **Selectivity** was added as the walk-forward and policy score. Σ % is trivially positive on a sleeve that loses in every era, so selectivity is the stricter test.
3. The Q3 pooled p is the N-weighted Δ against a within-7-day-block shuffle.
4. D1–D5 in `reports/ml_regime/diag.md` are **post-hoc** diagnostics: era-shift attribution, mix regression, BTC-after-entry anatomy, unconditional Q3, dose-response. They cannot promote anything.

**Multiplicity:**
- Screen: 600 tests per era × 3 eras.
- Pooled: 100 tests.
- Walk-forward: 300 directed tests.
- Q3: 40 cells.
- Policies: 5 rules × 3 blocks.

## Could NOT test

- **Open interest, liquidations, long/short ratio, order-book depth, 2025 funding.** Not cached, so the crowding / liquidity-provision explanation of "pops fade more in 2025" is untested.
- **The era-level cause itself.** Three eras are three observations, so it cannot be tested (WINDOW-UNITS rule).
- **Coin-level 15-minute forward return after entry.** The BTCFWD `fwd15` column equals BTC's, so the coin-response part was inferred from the intercept rather than measured directly.
- **Slot and portfolio effects of skipped fills.** Skips are priced as 0 in a fixed BT sequence.
- **Live regime state for the fresh cohort.** It spans 2 weeks (≈ 1–2 windows), so it is refute-only.
