Research only. Nothing in the bot, config, tests or UI changed. Pre-registration: `reports/SQUEEZER_2026-11-18_PREREG.md`
(frozen 13:10 UTC, before any per-variable or tradable result). Tables: `reports/SQUEEZER_2026-11-18_clusters.csv` (108 stop-cluster switch tests), `reports/SQUEEZER_2026-11-18_cells.csv` (every exit cell ×

## The question
After a Frenzy coin's second peak, the typical coin keeps falling, but a minority squeezes hard first and wipes out the profit.
1. Can we tell, at the entry, which coins will squeeze? Then we short only the "dumpers".
2. If not, can a tiny size with no stop harvest the median drift and survive the tail?
The same lens is applied to EXHAUSTION_SHORT entries.

## Plain answer
- **No usable squeeze predictor.** 42,235 tests (every entry variable incl. open interest, funding, long/short, taker flow, spot,
  age, market cap and the historical ORDER BOOK; 1D and every 2D pair) on three entry sets. Second-peak RSI (SP15): **zero** tests beat
  the shuffled-label null. Second-peak K (SP8): 25 beat the shuffle, 3 also beat the day-clustered null; the best (low 24 h move +
  bid-heavy order book) cuts the squeeze rate 33 % → 21 % in DESIGN but is **29 % vs 29 % (2023-24) and 28 % vs 30 % (since July)** in the two
  holdouts — it vanished. EXHAUSTION: 4 beat the shuffle (2 also the day-clustered null, neither holds its sign by half-year); the one that holds its direction is too weak to change P&L.
- **Filtering on the best candidates does not make a profitable short.** The best "dumpers only" rules look great in DESIGN and
  fail both holdouts: SP8 low-move + bid-heavy book +1.34 %/trade (DESIGN) → −4.05 % (2023-24) / −0.43 % (since July 2026); SP8 low
  volume-decay + bid-heavy book +2.79 % → −6.12 % / −0.25 %; SP8 low-move + low OI÷mcap +1.10 % → −1.14 % / −0.18 %.
- **Sizing alone does not work either.** The drift you see in the median is real, but the mean is ≈ 0 before costs and **negative
  after funding**: squeezed coins charge shorts heavy funding (−1.0 % per trade over 24 h, −1.8 % over 72 h on SP15). Net 72 h mean:
  SP15 −2.4 %, SP8 −1.3 %. At 5 % of equity per trade, the 72 h version has a 47–70 % chance of a −50 % drawdown within a year.
- **Verdict: NO** for a second-peak short, filtered or sized. One weak EXHAUSTION observe line (below), no arm.

## Why the median lies here
The median trade makes +5 to +10 %. The mean is ≈ 0 because the worst 5 % of trades carry about half of all losses, and single
trades lose 280–980 % (a coin that goes 3–10× after the entry). Funding then turns ≈ 0 into a loss. A strategy is paid on the
mean, not the median.

## Method (short)
- **Entries (frozen, from SECOND_PEAK_2026-11-18):** SP15 = `P2|P15|R0|RSI` (947 entries), SP8 = `P2|P8|R0|K` (3,573; the operator's
  rule), EXH = EXHAUSTION_SHORT realisable entries from EXH_EXIT_GRID (4,140). Entry price = the 5m open after the signal.
- **Labels (72 h path, 5m bars):** SQZ_X = price reaches +X % BEFORE −10 % (X = 10, 20, 50; primary 20). DUMPER = −10 % before
  +10 %. A bar touching both counts as a squeeze.
- **Blocks:** DESIGN 2024-07 → 2026-06 (screen + exit pick). HOLDOUT-A 2023-07 → 2024-06 (seen in aggregate by earlier studies).
  HOLDOUT-B 2026-07 → 11-17 (never read per variable before this study). A clean 2023-H1 holdout is **not buildable** (5m cache
  starts 2023-05).
- **Screen:** every variable at sign / median / terciles / theory cuts, and every 2D pair at median × median and tercile × tercile.
  Statistic = squeeze rate in the bucket vs the rest (z), bucket N ≥ 30. Null = 500 label shuffles (family-wise max |z|) and 300
  circular time shifts (keeps same-day clustering — the window-units rule). Survivor = shuffle p < 0.05, same direction in all four
  design half-years, no pair or day > 50 % of the bucket's squeezers.
- **Tradable test:** for the top 3 survivors per set, skip the predicted squeezers, 36 exits (TP 5 / 10 / none × stop 10 / 15 /
  2nd high + 0.5 % / none with a 50 % catastrophe cap × hold 24 / 48 / 72 h); exit picked on DESIGN mean only. Taker 0.05 % +
  slip 0.10 % per taker leg (0.20 stress), maker TP 0.02 % filling only 1 tick through, gap-aware stops, stop-first on a bar that
  touches both, real funding (hourly coins included, missing months −0.05 %/8 h), one position per pair.
- **Sizing route:** all entries, no stop, hold 24 / 72 h, net. Daily P&L at f = 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 % of equity notional per trade,
  7-day block bootstrap of days, 2,000 one-year paths.

**Reading.** Three themes repeat. (0) **A bid-heavy order book** (ask notional within +5 % ÷ bid notional within −5 % low) goes with
FEWER squeezes in SP8 — the strongest new variable, and it passes both nulls with a calm 24 h move or a low volume decay. It does not
replicate (holdout table). (a) **High ATR** coins squeeze more often (SP8). That is partly mechanical: a fixed +20 % is easier to
reach for a more volatile coin, and the same coins also dump harder, so the 24 h return barely differs. (b) **High open interest
relative to market cap** squeezes more (EXH, and its mirror in SP8: low OI ÷ mcap squeezes less). This matches the theory: a large
leveraged position against a small float is fuel for a squeeze. But both themes fail out of sample on P&L (next table), and OI ÷ mcap
rests on today's supply. Funding and long/short crowding — the classic squeeze story — do **not** separate at the family-wise level.

## What it means
- The second-peak short is a short-volatility trade: many small wins, rare huge losses. Its median is attractive; its mean is not.
- Nothing measured at entry — open interest, funding, long/short crowding, taker flow, spot share, coin age, market cap, BTC regime,
  order book — predicts the squeezers well enough to survive out of sample. The themes that look real in DESIGN (a bid-heavy book,
  volatility, OI vs float) either do not change P&L or disappear in both holdouts (book: squeeze rate 29 % vs 29 % and 28 % vs 30 %).
- This is the expected shape of an overfit: three SP8 rules at +1.1 … +2.8 %/trade in DESIGN, all negative in both holdouts.
- Funding is the hidden cost: 84 % of SP15 entries are already on hourly funding at entry. A 72 h short pays ≈ 1.8 % on average.
- EXHAUSTION_SHORT's tight 5 % stop is what makes it survivable; widening it or removing the TP (exit grid) brings the squeeze
  tail back.
