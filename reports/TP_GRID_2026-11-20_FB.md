## 5. Decomposition: where a wider TP gains and loses (Δ vs −5, stop +10, Σ pts)

| | ran on to the wider TP | hit −5, then stopped | hit −5, then timed out | stopped in both / unchanged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **−6**, backtest DESIGN | 68 · +66.1 | 1 · −15.6 | 2 · −8.7 | 23 · 0 |
| **−6**, backtest HOLDOUT | 313 · +300.4 | 11 · −168.8 | 7 · −40.1 | 120 · 0 |
| **−6**, real | 24 · +23.7 | 1 (CLANKER) · −15.3 | 0 | 2 · 0 |
| **−7**, backtest DESIGN | 61 · +111.6 | 4 · −61.9 | 6 · −14.1 | 23 · 0 |
| **−7**, backtest HOLDOUT | 289 · +554.7 | 23 · −354.4 | 19 · −81.0 | 120 · 0 |
| **−7**, real | 24 · +45.9 | 1 (CLANKER) · −15.3 | 0 | 2 · 0 |

How it works:
- A wider TP earns +1 to +2 pts on the large majority of winners, which keep falling well past −5 %.
- It pays ~15 pts on each winner that touched −5 and then squeezed back through +10 (the "second pump" from DIST_FB §4.2).
- At −6 that trade-off is ~13 : 1 in count. At −7 it is ~6 : 1, which is why −7's variance and drawdown are larger.
- Nothing about losers changes. A loser that never reached −5 is stopped the same way under every TP.

## 6. Live-cost notes

- The TP is a resting maker order (no slippage). The "through ≥ 0.05 %" rule removed no TP fill at −5 in either sample.
- **Funding matters** on real fills. Shorts paid up to −1.8 % on POWR and −0.7 % on KAT while held. A wider TP holds longer and pays more funding. This is included: it is why POWR books +3.13, not +4.93.
- Stop slippage of 0.25 % was applied to real fills; the backtest uses per-trade real-slip draws (mean 0.24 %). Paper stops filled at or better than the line (MMT, PHAROS).
- Depth: a wider TP does not change the entry, so the exit-side depth cap is unchanged. A tighter stop would add market orders on thin books.

## 7. What it means (plain English)

- **The stop is right.** Flatbase winners often go 6–8 % against us first, then fall. A tighter stop kills those winners. The real losers ran far past any stop we tested (MMT +62 % after its stop). Moving the stop only changes how much we lose on them, never whether. +12 helps a few trades in some periods and hurts in others.
- **The TP is probably a little too tight, but we can't prove it yet.** Most Flatbase winners keep falling past −5 %. Taking −6 % instead of −5 % adds about +0.2 %/trade in the backtest, in both the design and holdout eras. The 27 real fills point the same way. But when you test 49 combinations, one of them always looks better by luck. This gain is not big enough to beat that luck test, and it shrinks to about +0.12–0.17 %/trade after the usual haircut.
- **The 49-cell winners are search luck.** −8 / +10 (real) and −7 / +12 (backtest) look best. Remove a handful of the best trades, or test the DESIGN pick on HOLDOUT, and the edge disappears.

## 9. Pre-registered OBSERVE line (proposal; the operator decides, nothing armed)

**FB_TP6_SHADOW:** on every FLATBASE_SHORT fill, record whether a −6 % TP would have filled (through ≥ 0.05 %) before the +10 stop / 72 h, and its net vs the live −5. The fill's own 1m path is free to read after close.
- **Frozen gate (no re-fit):** after **≥ 30 new real fills in ≥ 15 windows**, ship TP −6 only if the paired Δ vs −5 is > 0 with **one-sided 95 %** window-bootstrap confidence **and** leave-best-5 %-out > 0.
- **Revert gate if shipped:** revert to −5 if the first 20 fills under −6 show **≥ 3 "hit −5, then stopped" fills**, or Σ Δ vs the −5 shadow < −10 pts.

## 10. Blind spots

- 15 of the 27 real fills (B24 / B25) use a modelled confirm entry (+0.25 % drift), not a real fill. Their DL 601 funding status comes from the original stamp.
- Backtest fills are 5m paths with modelled slippage. 1m-level intrabar order is not resolved there (stop-first on 7 same-bar cells).
- Funding at the real fills uses settlement rates × notional at entry (no mark-price adjustment, < 0.05 % effect at these sizes).
- A stop / TP sized by ATR was not tested (outside the question). Neither were partial exits.
